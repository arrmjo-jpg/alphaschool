# BUS-0032: `Term` Introduced as a Real Master Data Entity, Child of Academic Year

**Status:** 🟢 Accepted

**Date:** 2026-07-28 (revised twice same day — first after product-requirement confirmation reversed the original draft's conclusion, then after review added the stored `academic_year_id` column and its Consistency Invariant)

**Related Domains:** Academic, Learning, Students

**Related ADRs:** BUS-0011 (Course Offering's own Academic-Year-scoping precedent — considered, but not followed here, see Alternatives Considered), BUS-0018 (defines the Subject Offering formula this ADR resolves an ambiguity within), BUS-0019 (Teacher Assignment attaches to Subject Offering, a downstream beneficiary of this resolution)

## Context

A read-only dependency analysis of the remaining Academic roadmap (Timetables, Attendance, Subject Offering, Teacher Assignment, Grades, Branch Transfer — conducted 2026-07-28, following the same discipline as the Sections Architecture Pass) surfaced a genuine, previously unflagged gap: BUS-0018's own defining formula for Subject Offering — *"Subject × Term × Section × Teacher × Room × Schedule"* — includes an axis, "Term," that is never established as its own Master Data entity anywhere, never confirmed as a synonym for Academic Year or a real sub-year period (semester/quarter/trimester), and does not appear in any of Academic's own three Master Data enumerations (`academic.md:27`, `:127`, `:138` all list Academic Year, Grade Level, Section, Grading Scale — never Term). This blocks a correct design for Subject Offering itself, and transitively for Timetables (schedules against Subject Offering) and Teacher Assignment (attaches to Subject Offering).

**This ADR's first draft (same day) proposed resolving "Term" to `AcademicYear` directly — no new entity — reasoning from Learning's own `Course Offering` (BUS-0011) precedent, which scopes to Academic Year with no Term layer.** Before accepting that draft, the question was correctly identified as a *product* decision, not a technical one, and put to the party who actually holds that knowledge: **does the target market/near-term roadmap have a confirmed requirement for semester-level subject scheduling?** The answer was explicit: **yes — two or three terms per year are required soon**, for the actual schools this product targets. That single fact reverses the original draft's conclusion, per this ADR's own stated fallback ("if a real deployment need for sub-year subject scheduling is later confirmed... that is a new, separate architectural decision — introducing a real `Term` entity"). This is that decision, made now rather than deferred, because the need is confirmed now, not hypothetical.

A related, adjacent question — Academic Calendar's separation from Academic Year — is already tracked as Open Architecture Question #10 in `docs/BUSINESS_BLUEPRINT.md`, still 🟡 Proposed. This ADR addresses it only to the extent needed to confirm it does not block Subject Offering; it does not resolve it.

## Problem

What does "Term" mean in BUS-0018's Subject Offering formula, given a confirmed near-term business requirement for two-or-three-term-per-year scheduling — and how should `Term` relate to the already-frozen `AcademicYear` and `Section` (which stays year-scoped, not term-scoped, per its own already-Accepted decision)?

## Alternatives Considered

- **Term resolved to `AcademicYear` directly, no new entity** (this ADR's own original same-day draft, informed by Learning's `Course Offering`/BUS-0011 precedent). **Rejected upon product-requirement confirmation** — that precedent was sound reasoning *only* under the "no confirmed need" premise it was explicitly conditioned on; once semester-level scheduling was confirmed as a near-term requirement for actual target customers, the premise no longer holds, and following the precedent anyway would mean shipping Subject Offering against a structure known in advance to need reworking within the same commercial timeframe — the exact "solve half the problem now, redesign later" outcome this whole dependency analysis exists to avoid.
- **Term as a loose, free-text field on Subject Offering** (e.g., a plain string like `"Semester 1"`). Rejected, same reasoning as the original draft — unenforced, unversioned, inconsistent with the rigor Addendum A4 already demands for comparable period concepts, and now additionally wrong because a real Term needs its own `start_date`/`end_date` for scheduling/conflict logic, which a string cannot carry.
- **Section becomes Term-scoped instead of Year-scoped** (i.e., revisit the already-Accepted Sections decision to nest Section under Term rather than Academic Year directly). Rejected — Section's own scoping (`branch_id`, `academic_year_id`, `grade_level_id`) is already Accepted, independently reviewed, and shipped (`v1.7-academic-sections`); a class roster ("Grade 5 Section A") does not naturally change every semester the way a Subject Offering's own teacher/schedule might, and there is no confirmed requirement forcing Section itself to be re-scoped. Term attaches to Subject Offering, not to Section — Section stays exactly as already built.
- **Resolving Academic Calendar and Term together as one combined decision.** Rejected, same reasoning as the original draft — genuinely different concerns; forcing them into one ADR risks under-designing one or over-scoping this one.
- **`SubjectOffering.academic_year_id` as a derived-only value** (no stored column, always computed via `term.academic_year_id`) — this ADR's own immediately-prior draft. **Revised** — nearly every real query against `SubjectOffering` filters by year directly ("all subject offerings for year 2027"), not by walking through `Term` first; a stored, indexable `academic_year_id` keeps those queries, indexes, and reports simpler and faster. The cost (one denormalized column) is paid for with an explicit Consistency Invariant, not left as a hidden risk — the same trade-off already accepted elsewhere in this codebase (`Enrollment`/`Section` both store `branch_id` and `academic_year_id` as siblings, not one derived from the other).

## Final Decision

1. **`Term` is a new, real Master Data entity, a child of `AcademicYear`.** Fields: `academic_year_id` (FK, `restrictOnDelete()`, matching `Enrollment`/`Section`'s own convention), `name_en`/`name_ar` (bilingual — unlike `Section.name`, a Term's name ("First Semester" / "الفصل الدراسي الأول") is genuine translatable content, not a locale-agnostic label like "A"/"B"), `sequence_order` (unsigned int, ordering within the year — unique *within* `academic_year_id`, not globally, since Term 1 of every year legitimately reuses `sequence_order = 1`), `start_date`, `end_date`, and a **three-state `status` (`upcoming`/`active`/`closed`), mirroring `AcademicYear`'s own lifecycle exactly** — the identical reasoning `AcademicYear` was built on applies verbatim: Terms are created ahead of time before they start, and closing a Term should gate new writes scoped to it (e.g., no new Subject Offering rows against a closed Term), the same way `ClosedAcademicYearGuard` already gates writes against a closed year.
2. **`Section` is not re-scoped.** It remains exactly as already built and shipped — `(branch_id, academic_year_id, grade_level_id)`, per-year. `Term` attaches to `SubjectOffering`, not to `Section`.
3. **`SubjectOffering`'s own formula becomes `Subject × Term × Section × Teacher × Room × Schedule`, with `Term` a real FK (`term_id`), not a derived or synonymous value — and `SubjectOffering` also carries its own stored `academic_year_id` column directly, not derived-only via `term.academic_year_id`.** Revised from this ADR's own immediately-prior draft, which proposed `academic_year_id` as derived-only: nearly every real query against `SubjectOffering` (*"all subject offerings for year 2027"*) filters by year directly, not by walking through `Term` first — a stored, indexable `academic_year_id` keeps those queries, their indexes, and reporting simpler and faster, at the cost of one denormalized column whose consistency is enforced explicitly (Invariant below), the same trade-off already accepted for `Enrollment`/`Section` carrying `branch_id` and `academic_year_id` as siblings rather than requiring every reader to derive one from the other.
4. **Consistency Invariant (new, formal):** `SubjectOffering.term_id` must belong to `SubjectOffering.academic_year_id` — concretely, `Term.academic_year_id` (the `Term` referenced by `term_id`) must equal `SubjectOffering.academic_year_id` itself. Enforced in `SubjectOffering`'s own creation path (mirroring exactly how `SectionAssignment`'s own Consistency Invariant is enforced in its `booted()` hook, Phase 5 Sprint B — not left as an implicit consequence of "it references Term"), raising a dedicated `TermAcademicYearMismatchException` on violation, matching the "module/aggregate performing the check owns the exception" precedent already used throughout this Phase. This is the same "two independently-stored, denormalized-for-good-reason values must agree" pattern `SectionAssignment` already proved, applied one level up: `SubjectOffering` now independently stores both `term_id` and `academic_year_id`, and the Invariant is what keeps them from silently drifting apart, not an assumption about caller discipline.
5. **`Academic Calendar`'s separation from Academic Year (Open Architecture Question #10) is still explicitly NOT resolved by this ADR** — unchanged from the original draft. `Term`'s own `start_date`/`end_date` are a real, versioned answer to "when does this Term run," independent of whatever Academic Calendar eventually turns out to be (a calendar of holidays/significant dates *within* a Term or Year, most likely) — the two remain adjacent, not merged.
6. **`Term` is not branch-scoped**, matching `AcademicYear`'s own institution-wide scope — no confirmed requirement names branch-specific term calendars; if one surfaces later, that is its own future ADR, not assumed here.

## Why This Decision Was Chosen

The original draft's reasoning (reuse Learning's `Course Offering` precedent, avoid building ahead of a confirmed need) was sound *conditional on* no real semester requirement existing. Once the actual product owner confirmed one does — for real, near-term target customers, not a hypothetical future one — continuing to treat Term as a synonym for Academic Year would be knowingly shipping the wrong structure, which is a materially different situation from the "promotion, not prediction" caution the original draft correctly applied when the need was still unconfirmed. Addendum B1's own discipline cuts both ways: don't build ahead of a real need, but also don't withhold a real, already-confirmed one just because an earlier analysis (correctly, at the time) recommended against building it speculatively.

Mirroring `AcademicYear`'s own three-state lifecycle for `Term` (rather than inventing a new pattern, or using `Section`'s simpler `is_active` boolean) follows this project's own established discipline of reusing an existing, proven shape for a new entity that is structurally the same *kind* of thing — a Term is fundamentally a time period with the same "created ahead of time, eventually closed, gates writes once closed" behavior `AcademicYear` already has, not a plain reference/structural entity like `GradeLevel`/`Branch`/`Section`.

## Consequences

**Easier:** Subject Offering's own future architecture pass can proceed with a real, versioned, deployment-accurate scoping structure from day one — no rework anticipated within the current commercial timeframe, which was the entire point of running this dependency analysis before implementing anything.

**Harder / constrained:** `Term` is now one more Master Data entity every `SubjectOffering`-adjacent design (Timetables, subject-level Teacher Assignment, Grades' real attachment) must account for from the start, rather than a simpler `academic_year_id`-only scoping. `SubjectOffering` now carries two independently-stored scoping columns (`term_id`, `academic_year_id`) whose agreement the Consistency Invariant above must actively enforce, not assume. `ClosedAcademicYearGuard`'s own reasoning must be either extended or duplicated once `Term`'s own closed-state write-gating is implemented — **recorded guidance for that future design, not decided here:** prefer a single generalized guard (e.g. `AcademicPeriodGuard`, accepting either an `AcademicYear` or a `Term`) over a second near-duplicate `ClosedTermGuard` sitting beside `ClosedAcademicYearGuard` — the same "one shared mechanism, not one copy per consumer" discipline `HasTemporalAssignment` (Sprint A0) was hardened around, rather than letting closed-period guards start multiplying the way per-model overlap checks once did. This remains a real design decision for the eventual Subject Offering Architecture Pass, not finalized here, but named with a clear directional preference so it isn't decided from scratch later.

## Future Implications

Establishes that `Term`, not `AcademicYear`, is the correct period-scoping unit for `SubjectOffering` and everything that attaches to it (subject-level Teacher Assignment, period-level Attendance if that design is chosen, Grades' real attachment) — while `Section` and `Enrollment` both remain `AcademicYear`-scoped, unchanged. Future designs in this cluster must be explicit about which of the two scoping units they use and why, not assume one or the other by default.

Supersedes this ADR's own same-day original draft (the "Term = AcademicYear" conclusion) — recorded here, not silently deleted, per this project's own Architecture Assumptions discipline: an assumption stands until confirmed or replaced by a new ADR documenting the correction. In this case, both drafts live in the same document's revision history, since the reversal happened before the original was ever Accepted.

Unblocks the "Subject Offering + Timetables" Architecture Pass named in the Phase 5 dependency analysis to proceed on this corrected basis.
